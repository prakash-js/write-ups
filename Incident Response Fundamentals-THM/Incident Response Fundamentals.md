# Task 1 Introduction

**Security Incident :** A cybersecurity incident is a security event that compromises, or has the potential to compromise, the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an organization's systems, data, or services and requires a response from the security team.

**Incident Response :** It is a structured approach used by an organization to detect, respond to, and recover from security incidents while minimizing damage and business impact.

# Task 2 What Are Incident?

**Event :**</br>
An event is any activity or action that occurs on a system, application, or network. Examples include user logins, file access, process execution, network connections, and software installations. Events are continuously generated by operating systems, applications, and devices, and they serve as the raw source of security and operational information.

**Logs :**</br>
Logs are records of events that have occurred on a system or device.
They provide detailed information such as the time of the event, the user involved, the source system, and the action performed. Security teams use logs to monitor activity, investigate incidents, perform forensic analysis, and identify suspicious behavior.


**Alert :**</br>
An alert is a notification generated by a security tool, such as a SIEM, EDR, IDS, or IPS, when it detects activity that matches a predefined rule, pattern, or threat indicator. Alerts help security analysts focus on potentially suspicious events that may require further investigation.


**True Positive :**</br>
A true positive occurs when a security tool correctly identifies malicious or unauthorized activity, and the analyst confirms that the threat is real. For example, if an alert detects a phishing email and the investigation confirms that the email contains a malicious attachment, the alert is considered a true positive.

**False Positive :**</br>
A false positive occurs when a security tool generates an alert for activity that appears suspicious but is actually legitimate. For example, a large volume of data being transferred to a cloud storage service during a scheduled backup may trigger an alert, but after investigation, it is determined to be normal business activity.

# Task 3 Types of Incidents

Security incidents can occur in many forms, and not every incident is the same. Different incidents affect different aspects of an organization's security, such as confidentiality, integrity, and availability. Understanding the various types of incidents helps security teams identify threats, assess their impact, and respond effectively.

**Malware Infection :**</br>
A malware infection occurs when malicious software is installed on a system without authorization. Malware can steal data, damage files, provide unauthorized access to attackers, or disrupt normal operations. Common types include viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware, and spyware. Malware infections often originate from phishing emails, malicious downloads, or exploited vulnerabilities.

**Security Breach :** </br>
A security breach occurs when an unauthorized individual gains access to sensitive systems, networks, or data. The primary concern in a security breach is the loss of confidentiality. Security breaches can result in data theft, financial loss, legal consequences, and reputational damage to an organization.

**Data Leak :** </br>
A data leak is the exposure of sensitive or confidential information to unauthorized parties. Unlike some security breaches, data leaks can occur accidentally due to misconfigurations, human errors, or improper access controls. Examples include publicly exposed databases, accidentally shared documents, or leaked customer records.

**Insider Attack :** </br>
An insider attack occurs when someone within the organization, such as an employee, contractor, or partner, intentionally misuses their authorized access to harm the organization. Because insiders already possess legitimate access to systems and data, these attacks can be difficult to detect and may cause significant damage.

**Denial of Service (DoS) Attack :**</br>
A Denial of Service attack is an attempt to make a system, application, or network unavailable to legitimate users by overwhelming it with excessive traffic or requests. The goal is to exhaust system resources and disrupt business operations. When multiple systems are used to launch the attack, it is known as a Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attack.
